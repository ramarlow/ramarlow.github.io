For the final project in our robot controls class, 3 teammates and I built and programmed a haptic interface for rendering force feedback based on a 5-bar parallel linkage. Our goal was to simulate the feeling of pushing against stiff and compliant walls of a virtual shape, while also giving minimal resistance in free space. We designed a parallel mechanism based on the 5-bar or "pantograph" linkage, a pretty common approach in planar haptic devices. The two joints at the base were driven by Dynamixel servos and the proximal links were instrumented with load cells to get a secondary reading of the forces applied to the end effector.

We computed the Jacobian of the manipulator and compared it to results from literature, then tried implementing a couple of the different controllers that the class had covered, including an impedance controller (fairly successful) and an admittance controller (less successful). Among the project requirements was taking some time to manually tune controller gains, which helped us subjectively improve the feel of the rendered walls.

Although we had the option of running the controllers on an Arduino, we opted to just write a Python script and run it off a laptop, which allowed us to iterate a bit quicker with live control of gains and plotted trajectories. However, we didn't really consider what this would do for our control bandwitdh, and one of the bigger unexpected breakthroughs that improved our response later on was reconfiguring the USB port to decrease serial communication timeout so we could read and write faster.
